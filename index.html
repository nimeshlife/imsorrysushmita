page 01 · what i need to say

I’m sorry for how I handled it.

I want to own the hurt I caused, not just explain what was going on in my head.

♡

Hey Sushmita,

I’m sorry for the way I spoke to you today. I know I was rude, and I hate that a conversation between us ended with you crying and feeling anxious. You deserved patience from me, even while we disagreed. I didn’t give you that.

I know the photoshoot situation already made you feel insecure. I had told you I wouldn’t go again, and when the photographer reached out, I said I was sick instead of being clear that I didn’t want to go. I can understand why that left room for doubt. I should have communicated honestly and clearly instead of putting off the uncomfortable conversation.

I was frustrated because I felt that my accountability was being questioned, and I reacted to that feeling instead of slowing down and listening to yours. I may still see parts of the situation differently, but that doesn’t excuse my tone, or the way I made you feel while trying to make my point.

I’m especially sorry if I made you feel like I was ready to walk away from us. I don’t want our disagreements to leave you wondering whether our relationship is safe every time things get difficult. I should never use my anger or the threat of leaving to make a moment more painful.

I love you, and I want to get better at showing that when I’m upset—not only when everything is fine. I can’t fix today with a webpage, and I don’t expect these words to make the hurt disappear. I just want to start by acknowledging it properly.

I’m sorry, my love.
Nimesh

page 04 · not just words

What I want to do better.

✳

Be clear, even when it’s uncomfortable.

If I don’t want to do something, I’ll communicate that honestly instead of leaving things vague and hoping the problem goes away.

✳

Listen before I defend myself.

I want to understand what’s making you anxious before I explain my side. Your feelings deserve to be heard, even when we see a situation differently.

✳

Take responsibility for my tone.

Being frustrated doesn’t give me permission to speak to you rudely. I want to pause, calm down, and return to the conversation with more care.

✳

Make my actions match my words.

I know trust grows through consistency, not one long message. I need to show you the change over time, without expecting instant reassurance or forgiveness.

I know “I’ll improve” only means something when you can actually see me trying.
